The design question

How might a financial plan create confidence before complexity?

Financial products often equate sophistication with dense terminology. FinWell starts with the questions clients actually ask: What is available, what is planned, what am I building toward, and what could I change?

01

Frame trust needs

Identify the moments that require transparency: value origin, allocation tradeoffs, uncertainty, privacy, and advisor handoff.

02

Write the money story

Translate categories and projections into clear, neutral language before deciding chart forms.

03

Layer the numbers

Lead with a small summary, then allow users to inspect allocation, goals, and scenarios at their own pace.

04

Prototype tradeoffs

Make adjustments immediately visible while showing when a plan no longer balances.

Key decisions

Trust is a product feature.

Good financial UX does not promise certainty. It shows source, scope, assumptions, and a responsible path to human help.

01

Plain language before finance vocabulary

Labels describe the user’s intent—essentials, goals, flexible choices—while deeper explanations remain available.

02

Privacy is one visible action away

Amount masking supports public or shared-screen contexts without logging out or changing the underlying plan.

03

Scenarios disclose their limits

The what-if control is explicitly illustrative, avoiding false precision or a disguised recommendation.

04

Humans stay in the service model

An advisor path remains visible for questions that require context, consent, or professional judgment.

Validation plan

Test comprehension before conversion.

A responsible study would prioritize what users believe the numbers mean, where they came from, and what is or is not guaranteed.

Task 01

Explain the monthly plan

Can participants accurately describe available money and the three allocation categories?

Task 02

Explore a scenario

Do users understand that changing the contribution is an illustration rather than advice?

Task 03

Find human support

When a decision exceeds the dashboard’s scope, can they identify the appropriate next step?
