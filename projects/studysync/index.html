The design question

How might one workspace protect a student’s attention while keeping collaboration visible?

Student tools often reward activity rather than progress. StudySync reframes the dashboard around a realistic day: a few priority tasks, scheduled focus, and the team context needed to keep momentum.

01

Organize the ecosystem

Consolidate tasks, scheduling, group work, email notifications, and resources under one predictable navigation model.

02

Prioritize the day

Make the “today” view useful before asking students to manage an entire academic system.

03

Reduce attention debt

Use calm defaults, a small priority list, and an explicit focus state to minimize context switching.

04

Design every state

Prototype completion, progress, timer, dialog, schedule selection, and empty-state behavior.

Key decisions

Momentum over busyness.

The product uses progress as information, not pressure. The UI surfaces the next meaningful action while leaving space for recovery and changing plans.

01

One primary “today” surface

Instead of opening on a full calendar or task backlog, the product starts with a manageable daily picture.

02

Time and tasks share context

The schedule and priority list appear together so students can judge whether a plan is realistic.

03

Focus mode is visible and reversible

The timer makes state obvious, gives a simple pause/reset path, and avoids gamifying time with punitive streaks.

04

Collaboration without notification overload

Teams remain visible in the navigation, while notifications use quiet hierarchy until attention is actually required.

Validation plan

Test whether planning feels lighter.

A production study would recruit students with different course loads and accessibility needs, testing comprehension before measuring adoption.
